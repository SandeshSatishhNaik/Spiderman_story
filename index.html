Act I

The Street

EXT. Rooftop above 8th Avenue - Night

11:47 p.m. Twelve floors above Eighth Avenue.

From up here the city is mostly light and noise. A cab horn. Somebody laughing outside a bar on 44th.

Then a siren turns the corner, and I stop hearing anything else.

Act II

The Lens

Extreme close-up - The lens

Through the lenses, the street breaks into a thousand small windows.

I made them to cut the glare. They also cut out the part of me that wants to go home.

Spider-sense has no sound. It's a pressure at the base of my skull that makes the call before I do.

Tonight it hasn't let up since sundown.

Act III

The Mask

EXT. Ledge, Hell's Kitchen - Continuous

People think the mask is there so they can't see me.

Mostly it's there so I can't see the kid from Queens with a chem test at eight.

Someone I loved told me that power comes with a bill.

Tonight it's due on Eighth Avenue.

Vol. LXIV New York's finest daily newspaper

Wall-Crawler Stalks Eighth Ave.

Masked vigilante seen crouched above Hell's Kitchen for a third straight night. Police report no crime. The publisher says that is exactly what worries him.

By Ben Urich, Staff Reporter

Hell's Kitchen. For the third night running, residents of West 44th Street looked up and found the so-called Spider-Man looking back down. Witnesses put the masked figure on a ledge twelve floors above Eighth Avenue, motionless for long stretches, “like he was listening for something.”

Shortly before midnight a patrol car answered a call two blocks south. By the time officers arrived, the suspect had been webbed to a lamppost with a note attached. The ledge was empty.

The NYPD declined to say whether it is investigating the vigilante, the suspect or the lamppost.

Editorial

Threat or Menace?

J. Jonah Jameson, Publisher

Readers write in every week asking why this newspaper never prints a kind word about the web-slinger. Here is one: brief. He was brief. Swung in, swung out, and left a public lamppost glued shut for the taxpayers to scrape clean.

A hero shows his face. A hero files a report. A hero does not hide behind a mask and a fistful of string.

The Bugle will keep asking the question. You are welcome to keep getting the answer wrong.
